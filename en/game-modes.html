The four game modes

The GOAT offers four ways to play the same career: Career, Arcade Mode, GOAT Mode and Quick Mode. The engine underneath is identical: same simulation, same 30-team league, same final verdict. What changes is how much information you get and how long each career takes. The comparison below should make the pick easy.

Career: the default mode

The full experience, recommended for your first run. In the draft, you see every value of the drawn legend before choosing what to steal, including the price (malus) of each choice. Through the season you follow the schedule, step on the court in key games, resolve decision moments and make the career calls: contract offers, deadline trades, season focus, when to retire.

Length: 20 to 40 minutes for a full career, depending on how many games you watch.

For: anyone who wants the whole experience and full control over the build.

Arcade Mode: key games become minigames

The same career as Career, with one difference on the court: every decisive moment in key games and playoffs is a minigame in 3D, camera low over the player's shoulder, instead of a pick between two or three options. There are three: the playbook (you draw routes for all five on a top-down tactical board, pick from 6 play templates or freehand your own, run the possession live against one of 5 hidden defensive schemes — man, zone, switch-everything, trap, pressure — and step in with a pass, a fake, a screen call or a shot fake before deciding when to let it go; the play freezes whenever nobody is moving, and the shot clock only runs while it is live), the shot (camera behind the shooter: pick a shot from your repertoire, beat the defender's closeout, aim with the two-tap meter or by dragging, with an arrow showing angle and power, and tap anywhere on the screen at the apex of the jump) and the wall (a continuous 1-on-1 duel by gesture — swipe to shadow the attacker, tap to gamble on the steal, swipe up to rise for the block — with a tendency card showing his odds to shoot, drive or pass). Every minigame opens full screen, on phone and desktop, behind a rules screen that explains what to do: nothing starts counting until you tap PLAY. Without 3D support the shot and the wall fall back automatically to a 2D version with the same controls (the playbook is always 2D anyway).

Your execution in the minigame moves the play's odds by up to 25 percentage points, up or down. Attributes still rule the rest: a perfect release from a player with 55 shooting still misses plenty, and a 95 shooter makes it even with an average one. The play you choose also sets the risk, just like in Career: layup and assist are safe plays, threes and dunks carry the bigger prize.

Length: a bit longer than Career, since each moment takes 10 to 20 seconds.

For: anyone who wants their hands on the game, not just the decision. It is also the least predictable mode: the defense never does the same thing twice.

GOAT Mode: the blind steal

The same career, with a difficulty lock at the start: in the draft, the legend's values and the malus stay hidden until the end. You know who the legend is and choose what to steal in the dark, trusting what you know about basketball: the jumper of one of history's great scorers is probably high, but how high? And where will the malus hurt?

The result is a draft of instinct, where knowledge of the legends beats spreadsheets. Your build only reveals itself when the career begins, and then you play with what you got.

Length: same as Career.

For: veterans who've mastered the open draft and want to test their memory; basketball fans who enjoy betting on their own knowledge.

Quick Mode: a career in minutes

The laboratory mode. The draft is the same as Career (values visible), but the season runs by itself: no game screens and no decision moments. You only answer the career decisions that matter: offers, trades, retirement. A full career wraps in a few minutes.

Length: a few minutes per career.

For: testing build hypotheses back to back ("what if I go all defense?"), comparing attribute priorities, or hunting the GOAT verdict across many attempts, since every career draws a fresh set of legends.

Which one to pick?

  • First time? Career. The game was designed around it.
  • Want to actually play on the court? Arcade Mode.
  • Finished a few careers? GOAT Mode to renew the challenge.
  • Optimizing builds or short on time? Quick Mode.

A practical note: the game keeps one career at a time, and starting a new one replaces the current. The save lives in your browser and an ongoing career picks up where you left off when you reopen the page.

What stays the same across modes

  • The simulated league: 30 teams, award races, trades and playoffs.
  • The age curve and retirement.
  • The verdict math: the GOAT tier is equally demanding in all three.
  • Both languages (PT/EN), switchable any time.
