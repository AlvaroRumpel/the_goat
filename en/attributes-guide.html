Attributes guide

Every build in The GOAT is made of eight attributes on a 40-99 scale. They're not decorative: each one feeds specific parts of the simulation — points, rebounds, assists, awards, longevity and the decision moments. This guide shows what each attribute really does, so you steal with intent in the draft.

The scoring attributes

3PT Shooting

The number one engine of your scoring average — no attribute weighs more on points per game. It also defines the Shooting Guard archetype together with Clutch. If your plan is chasing scoring titles (26+ points per game), start here.

Finishing

The second biggest weight in scoring and one of the three pillars of the Center archetype. It also contributes to rebounding — players who finish at the rim fight in the paint. Pairs well with Physical in a power build.

Ball Handling

A hybrid attribute: it feeds scoring (creating your own shot) and is the second engine of assists, behind Passing. Scoring guards need it on both ends.

Clutch

The most underrated attribute in the game. It counts toward your scoring average, dominates the decision moments in key games — especially the final play — and raises your title probability in the playoffs. A 90+ Clutch build converts good seasons into rings; the difference shows up in the verdict.

The playmaking and defense attributes

Passing

The main engine of assists and half the identity of the Point Guard archetype. An average of 8+ assists earns an All-Star nod even without big scoring numbers.

Defense

The road to Defensive Player of the Year and one of the pillars of the Center archetype. It also boosts your team when you choose a defensive focus for the season. Defense doesn't fill the points column — it fills the trophy shelf.

Rebounding

The dominant engine of rebounds per game. Averaging 11+ boards earns All-Star selections on its own. Centers and power forwards live off it; point guards can sacrifice it guilt-free.

Physical

The attribute that works in silence. It contributes to rebounding and defines the Power Forward archetype together with Finishing — but its most valuable effect is longevity: the higher your Physical, the slower the age curve bites after 29. Careers of 15+ seasons chasing historic totals are built on high Physical.

The age curve

Every player rises to a peak, holds it, then declines:

  • Ages 19-25 — the climb: you play below your ceiling while maturing.
  • Ages 26-29 — the peak: your build performs at 100%.
  • 30+ — year-over-year decline, slower with high Physical, down to a floor of 60% of your peak.

That means peak seasons are few — plan to be on a competitive team when they arrive, because that's when MVPs and rings are realistic.

Benchmark targets

  • All-Star: 19+ points, or 8+ assists, or 11+ rebounds per game.
  • Scoring title: 26+ points per game puts you in the race.
  • Defensive Player of the Year: elite Defense, sustained across the season.
  • MVP: big numbers + a winning team. No single attribute delivers it — the whole build does.

Priorities by build project

  • Pure scorer: 3PT Shooting, Finishing, Ball Handling, Clutch. Sacrifices Rebounding and Passing.
  • Classic point guard: Passing, Ball Handling, Clutch. Sacrifices Rebounding and Physical — and accepts fewer points.
  • Defensive anchor: Defense, Rebounding, Physical, Finishing. Scores less, but collects DPOYs and All-Star rebounding.
  • GOAT hunter: high Clutch and 3PT for rings and points, high Physical to last 18+ seasons. The full path is in the verdict guide.
